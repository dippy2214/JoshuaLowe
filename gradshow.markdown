## Environmental Effects In Proximity Voice Chat

For my honours project I investigated the impact of simulating environmental effects in proximity voice chat audio. The project tested voice chat in a controlled environment by surveying participants experience both with and without the environmental effects in the audio. Results showed an increase in percieved immersion and engagement for participants when using environmental effects, as well as changes in behaviour while playing the game.

### What were the impacts?

As well as participants reporting more immersion and engagement, there was also themes of increased realism and enjoyment from the survey results. Unfortunately, results pertaining to communication clarity were inconclusive due to participants being able to hear others in the room during testing. However, perhaps the most interesting results were the observed behavioural changes.

Participants were seen utilizing strategies previously observed in proximity voice chat research, such as forming multiple small groups, strategising together and attempting to eavesdrop. However, the environmental effects created new behaviours on top of this. Some participants at the end of rounds began to play a "Marco, Polo" style game to search for each other and ambushing became a more common strategy, especially around changes in verticality in the terrain.

### What does this mean for my games?

To take advantage of this research in your own projects, the results suggest inital support for the idea of conceptualising voice chat audio as gameplay audio rather than just a communication tool. In practice this could mean design levels with communication lines in mind, using geometry to limit the flow of information between certain areas. Designers can also work under the understanding the voice information can be used to convey spatial information as well as vocal communication, potentially allowing them to clear up UI elements which would serve the same purpose.
